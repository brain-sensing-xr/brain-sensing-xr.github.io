Why this workshop

What happens when XR can sense the brain?

This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners in the mixed reality community to brainstorm how brain sensing can be useful for VR, AR and XR applications.

Brain sensing could help us understand cybersickness, presence and cognitive load in immersive environments. But translating neural measurements into real-world XR systems raises difficult questions around movement, hardware constraints, real-time analysis, privacy and responsible deployment.

Our aim is not just to share results, but to build a research vision that inspires collaboration and opens new directions for this emerging scientific frontier.

Topics of interest

Questions at the frontier

We welcome previously published work, emerging results and opinion or position contributions across topics including, but not limited to:

Sensing the brain during movement, in real time and with commodity devices
Methods, methodologies and theoretical constructs for brain sensing in XR
Lessons from other contexts that translate to immersive environments
New opportunities uniquely enabled by brain sensing in XR
Privacy and security risks of brain sensing in personal and enterprise XR
Applications such as cybersickness, presence and cognitive load

The final topic above provides application examples from the workshop overview; the other topics follow the call for contributions.

A blend of expert insight, emerging ideas and attendee-led discussion.

Poster exchange

Participants share published findings, early-stage research or position statements as seeds for discussion and collaboration.

Collaborative breakouts

Small-group discussions shaped by invited expertise and themes arising during the panel and poster session.

What to submit

We invite one-page abstracts about previously published work, emerging results, and opinions or positions. The CFP also specifies that position papers should be one to two pages in the IEEE VR poster submission format.

  • Prepare your abstract or position paper as a PDF.
  • For position papers, use the IEEE VR poster submission format.
  • Email your contribution to ejain@ufl.edu.
  • Use the exact email subject: VR 2027 Brain Sensing Workshop submission.
